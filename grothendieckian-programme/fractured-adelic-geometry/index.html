Abstract, as published

Fractured Adelic Geometry is the fourth work in the WiseMotif Monograph of Mathematical Intelligence. It belongs to the Grothendieckian Programme as a self-contained system and as one of the Programme's research subjects: a problem on which the method of recursive mathematical discovery is carried through in full, in the manner of Grothendieck's rising sea. The problem is the conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer, and the question is why sixty years of work have produced theorems only in analytic rank at most one, and what kind of object would make the conjecture a single statement instead of a comparison between two unrelated quantities.

The diagnosis is structural. Every proved case crosses from the analytic side to the arithmetic side by a bridge — Heegner points, Kolyvagin systems, Kato's Euler system — that carries one class in one direction, and the book isolates the rank-two obstruction as a statement about the exterior algebra acting on a derived deformation ring, not as a technical accident. The proposal changes the object. A category of Frobenius–Arakelov–Prismatic (FAP) spaces carries condensed analytic structure, integral prismatic F-gauges at every prime, Fargues–Fontaine realisations, an Arakelov boundary, an absolute cyclotomic structure and an arithmetic flow as foundational data, governed by twelve axioms F1–F12. To an elliptic curve E over Q it attaches an arithmetic spectrum X_E, a categorified Lefschetz–Verdier trace formula and a fundamental line Δ(X_E). The conjecture becomes the Master Identity (Conjecture A): the analytic and algebraic trivialisations of Δ(X_E) agree integrally.

The descent is proved: from Conjecture A and the axioms the rank formula and the full leading-coefficient formula follow. In the degenerate cases the framework reproduces known theorems: over a finite field nine of the twelve axioms become theorems and the formalism specialises to the theorem of Tate and Milne; for the multiplicative group over Z it specialises to the explicit formula and the class number formula. What remains is stated as a register and an atlas: twelve conjectures, of which eleven are rated at the two highest levels of difficulty and only one is tractable with present technique, and fourteen obstructions, five of which contain a known major open problem — Conjecture A is the equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture for h¹(E)(1) in the notation of the book. A seven-layer computational architecture with certified engines, a certificate hierarchy with a stated trust model, a proof-obligation ledger and a Lean 4 development of the statements, published uncompiled, complete the work.

Every substantive assertion carries one of four status labels — known, conditional, new, open — where it is made. The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture remains open. This work does not prove it, proves no case of it that was not already known, and proves the finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich group for no curve.
